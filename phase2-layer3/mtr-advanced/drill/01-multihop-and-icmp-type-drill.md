DRILL 1 — A ping succeeds with TTL=63 instead of the expected TTL=64.
What does that single-digit difference prove about the path?
YOUR ANSWER:
>
REFERENCE:
The packet passed through exactly one real router/hop -- TTL
decrements by 1 at each hop it traverses.

DRILL 2 — You want to build a genuine 2-hop path for testing, using
three namespaces (client, router1, server) on two different subnets.
What single setting, applied inside router1, is required for it to
actually forward traffic between its two interfaces (without it,
routing entries alone are not enough)?
YOUR ANSWER:
>
REFERENCE: sysctl -w net.ipv4.ip_forward=1

DRILL 3 — You rate-limit ICMP echo-request on an intermediate router's
INPUT chain, expecting mtr to show loss at that hop. It shows 0% loss
instead. What ICMP type does mtr/traceroute actually rely on for
intermediate-hop responses, and why does an echo-request rule miss it
entirely?
YOUR ANSWER:
>
REFERENCE:
ICMP time-exceeded (type 11). Traceroute/mtr send low-TTL packets;
each hop automatically generates a time-exceeded reply as a side
effect of TTL reaching zero -- it never sends or receives an
echo-request/echo-reply for intermediate hops at all.

DRILL 4 — You correctly target ICMP type 11 (time-exceeded) but apply
the rule to the router's INPUT chain, and it still has no effect. Why,
and which chain is actually correct?
YOUR ANSWER:
>
REFERENCE:
time-exceeded messages are GENERATED by the router itself and sent OUT
toward the sender -- this is OUTPUT traffic from the router's
perspective, never INPUT. Correct: iptables -I OUTPUT ...

DRILL 5 — Write the tcpdump command to directly observe which ICMP
types are actually crossing a namespace during an mtr run, to confirm
your assumption about ICMP type before writing a firewall rule.
YOUR ANSWER:
>
REFERENCE: sudo ip netns exec <namespace> tcpdump -i any -n icmp -c 20

DRILL 6 — mtr shows real, measurable loss at hop 1 but 0% loss at
every hop after it, including the final destination. What does this
combination prove, and what would it mean if hop 1 loss were instead
accompanied by loss at every subsequent hop too?
YOUR ANSWER:
>
REFERENCE:
Loss isolated to hop 1 alone, with clean hops after it, means hop 1 is
simply not reliably replying to probes about itself -- it IS still
forwarding real traffic (proven by hops after it succeeding). If loss
appeared at hop 1 AND every hop after it, that would suggest hop 1 is
genuinely failing to forward traffic, since nothing downstream could
be reached without passing through it first.
